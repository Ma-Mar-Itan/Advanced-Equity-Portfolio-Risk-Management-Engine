# QFI V2 presentation notes

The final deck is a 15-slide decision narrative generated by
`tools/build_deck_artifact.mjs`. Its charts and tables are native PowerPoint
objects backed by the validated snapshot in `report_assets/results.json`.

## 1. Advanced Equity Portfolio Risk Management

Open with the decision question: how much can the portfolio lose, when do the
models fail, and what should a risk manager do?

## 2. Portfolio and research design

Explain the workflow as collect, clean, model and challenge. The same settings
and calculations drive the dashboard, report and slides.

## 3. Data quality and return behavior

All six data checks pass with no gaps or stale values. Individual-stock
volatility varies substantially, while the portfolio is left-skewed and
fat-tailed. A normal distribution is therefore an unsafe loss assumption.

## 4. Volatility is clustered and asymmetric

Large negative days are followed by larger absolute moves over the next five
days. GJR-GARCH captures this through a positive leverage coefficient. The
estimated shock half-life is about 21 trading days.

## 5. Four risk models answer different questions

Historical Simulation is transparent but slow-moving. EWMA-HS reacts to recent
shocks. GARCH-HS adds mean reversion. GJR-HS also allows bad news to raise risk
more than good news of the same size.

## 6. Current one-day VaR and Expected Shortfall

At 99% confidence, GJR-HS estimates a one-day VaR of about $297,000 and an
Expected Shortfall of about $338,000. Expected Shortfall is the average loss on
days beyond the VaR threshold.

## 7. Confidence and window choice materially change VaR

The GJR-HS grid ranges from 1.34% to 3.34% of portfolio value. A risk limit must
therefore name the model, confidence level and estimation window, not simply
say “VaR.”

## 8. Backtesting asks three separate questions

Kupiec tests the exception rate. Christoffersen tests whether exceptions
cluster. Conditional coverage tests both together. Every forecast is made for
the next day using only information available at the time.

## 9. Exception counts look acceptable; clustering is the problem

All four models produce close to the expected 31.1 exceptions in 3,114
forecasts. Only GJR-HS passes all three tests at the 5% threshold; the other
models show statistically significant clustering.

## 10. GJR-HS ranks first in the 99% backtest

Use GJR-HS as the primary monitoring model, EWMA-HS as a responsive challenger,
and plain HS as the transparent benchmark. Ranking is based on the full test
suite, not on which model produces the smallest VaR.

## 11. Crisis losses overwhelm ordinary daily VaR

The 2008 and COVID scenarios imply losses of roughly 44% and 35%, far above
ordinary daily VaR. Daily VaR is a control metric, not a crisis-loss ceiling.

## 12. Risk is more concentrated than capital

Tesla and Amazon contribute about 37% of Expected Shortfall while representing
about 20% of capital. Equal capital weights do not create equal risk weights.

## 13. The analytics translate into concrete actions

Illustrative hedges materially reduce VaR, and the in-sample optimizer lowers
Expected Shortfall. These are decision aids, not automatic trades: liquidity,
turnover, basis risk and forecast controls must be added before execution.

## 14. What the framework does not solve

Be explicit about survivorship bias, square-root-of-time scaling, sample
sensitivity, scenario limitations, point-in-time shares, and independent data
reconciliation. Model risk should be governed rather than hidden.

## 15. Five conclusions

Close with the operating model: monitor with GJR-HS, challenge with EWMA-HS,
and govern with stress testing and attribution. The dashboard connects
measurement, validation and action in a single reproducible workflow.
